# Jurisdiction-Specific Requirements

Reference this file when the user specifies a non-EU jurisdiction or when the application serves
users in several regions. This material is informational and not legal advice; verify the current
law against official sources and record the consultation date in the report.

---

## United States — California: CCPA / CPRA

**Applies when:** the business collects personal information from California residents and meets the
statutory thresholds (for example, revenue above the statutory amount, processing the data of more
than 100,000 consumers or households, or deriving a majority of revenue from selling or sharing
personal information). The monetary threshold is adjusted over time; verify the current figure in
the official regulations.

**Key requirements on top of general best practices:**

- Disclose the categories of personal information collected in the last 12 months.
- Disclose the categories of third parties with which data is shared.
- Right to Know (categories and specific pieces).
- Right to Delete.
- Right to Opt-Out of Sale/Sharing ("Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link).
- Right to Correct.
- Right to Limit Use of Sensitive Personal Information.
- No discrimination for exercising rights.
- CPRA adds the Right to Correct, an expanded "sensitive personal information" definition and a
  data-minimization principle.

**Privacy policy should include:** a "California Privacy Rights" section or a separate California
privacy notice, contact information for rights requests, and the date of the last update.

## Brazil — LGPD (Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados)

**Applies when:** personal data of individuals in Brazil is processed, regardless of where the
controller is based.

**Key requirements:**

- Ten legal bases for processing (similar to GDPR Art. 6, including credit protection).
- Data-subject rights: access, correction, anonymization/deletion, portability, information about
  sharing, and withdrawal of consent.
- Appointment of a data protection officer (Encarregado); the ANPD may provide exemptions for small
  processing agents under its regulations — verify the current rule.
- Breach notification within a reasonable time (ANPD guidance has indicated a short deadline).
- International transfers only to adequate countries or through contractual safeguards.

**Differences from the GDPR:**

- Extraterritorial scope is based on where the data subjects are, not only on establishment.
- Legitimate interest has a narrower application.
- The right to complain to a supervisory authority does not take exactly the same form.

## China — PIPL (Personal Information Protection Law)

**Applies when:** personal information of individuals in China is processed.

**Key requirements:**

- Separate consent for sensitive personal information.
- Cross-border transfer requires a security assessment (CAC), certification or standard contracts,
  as applicable.
- A local representative or entity is required for certain overseas processors.
- Data-localization requirements apply to certain sectors (for example, critical information
  infrastructure operators).
- Purpose limitation is strictly enforced.

---

## United Kingdom — UK GDPR + Data Protection Act 2018

**Post-Brexit:** the UK GDPR is largely equivalent to the EU GDPR, with these differences:

- The ICO (Information Commissioner's Office) is the supervisory authority.
- An EU adequacy decision covers UK-to-EU transfers.
- UK-to-EU transfers are currently covered; monitor for changes.
- The UK has its own transfer instruments, including the International Data Transfer Agreement
  (IDTA) and the International Data Transfer Addendum.

## Australia — Privacy Act 1988 + Australian Privacy Principles

**Applies when:** annual turnover exceeds the statutory threshold, health records are handled, or
other statutory conditions apply.

**Key requirements (Australian Privacy Principles):**

- APP 1: Open and transparent management — a privacy policy is required.
- APP 5: Notification of collection.
- APP 6: Use or disclosure of personal information.
- APP 8: Cross-border disclosure.
- APP 11: Security of personal information.
- APP 12: Access to personal information.
- APP 13: Correction of personal information.

There is no strict consent requirement in all cases; a "reasonable expectations" standard applies.

## General best practices (jurisdiction-neutral)

When no specific jurisdiction is identified, audit against these universally accepted principles:

1. **Purpose limitation** — data is used only for the stated purposes.
2. **Data minimization** — only the data necessary for the purpose is collected.
3. **Storage limitation** — data is not kept longer than necessary.
4. **Transparency** — plain language, accessible policy.
5. **Security** — appropriate technical and organizational measures.
6. **Accountability** — a responsible party and documented processes exist.
7. **User control** — meaningful opt-out, access and deletion mechanisms.
